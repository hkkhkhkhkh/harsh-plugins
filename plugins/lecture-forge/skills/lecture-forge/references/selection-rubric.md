# What counts as a clip worth publishing

The curator agent applies this. It is here so the standard can be tuned in one
place, and so a human reviewing `review.md` knows what was being asked.

## Four tests, all of which must pass

**Self-contained.** A viewer who has not seen the previous forty minutes
understands it. Disqualifying: "as I mentioned", an unexplained "this", a
reference to a diagram drawn before the clip begins, a term defined earlier.

**One idea, carried to a payoff.** Setup, explanation, conclusion. A claim
asserted and never developed is not a clip, however true. Neither is three
loosely related ideas in sequence — that is three clips, or none.

**Substance the viewer could not have guessed.** A mechanism, a number, a named
study, a worked example, a distinction people commonly get wrong. Motivation,
encouragement, and restatements of the obvious are not substance.

**A usable opening.** The first ten seconds hook, or the start can be moved to a
line that does. Beginning on the claim beats beginning on the throat-clearing
before it.

## Never a clip

Administration. Repetition of covered material. Tangents that never land on a
teaching point. Audience questions whose audio cannot be relied on. Anything
that depends on a visual you cannot confirm was on screen.

## Boundaries

`start` is the first word of the setup, `end` the last word of the payoff. No
padding, and no stretching to reach a round number — a tight four minutes beats
a loose seven. Clip length follows the idea, which in practice lands anywhere
between about one and ten minutes.

**A clip that answers a question must contain the question.** When the selected
stretch is the payoff to something the speaker asked earlier, the start moves
back to that question however far back it sits, and the discussion in between
comes along. Audience guesses on the way to a reveal are the tension, not a
tangent. Opening on the answer alone discards the reason anyone would watch.

The check: does the first line tell a stranger what question is coming? If not,
the start is too late.

## Scoring

1–10, honestly spread. Scoring everything an 8 makes the review sheet useless,
because the whole point of the score is to tell the human what to look at first.

## Mistake cuts are a separate list

Removed from whatever survives, whether or not they sit inside a proposed clip:
false starts, abandoned sentences, self-corrections (cut the wrong version and
the correction phrase, keep the corrected statement), admin interruptions
mid-explanation, long unresolved digressions.

The test for a mistake cut: removing it must leave a grammatical, complete
sentence behind. When in doubt, leave it — a surviving stumble is a smaller
problem than a chopped sentence. Pauses, "um", and stuttered repeats are handled
mechanically in stage 2 and are not the curator's job.

## An empty window is a real answer

A ten-minute stretch with nothing publishable in it returns no clips. Promoting
weak material to fill the list is the failure mode this rubric exists to
prevent.
